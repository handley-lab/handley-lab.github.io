*Handley Lab Publishing & Authorship Policy. Adopted 28 August 2025.*

The Handley Lab values fair attribution of scientific contributions while maintaining a coherent group identity. This policy outlines our approach to authorship and publication practices.

## Authorship Criteria

Authorship requires **significant intellectual contribution** to the work. In our AI-accelerated research group, intellectual contribution means identifying what needs to be done and ensuring it happens correctly - whether through direct implementation or through skilled use of AI tools.

### AI-Accelerated Science

We value the intellectual work of problem formulation, solution design, and quality assurance over the mechanical act of implementation. Providing critical, timely, and precise instructions to AI/agentic systems that generate novel analyses or solutions represents significant intellectual contribution. What matters is:
- Identifying the right question or approach
- Directing AI systems with expertise and precision
- Ensuring correctness and validity
- Taking responsibility for the output

AI systems are tools, not authors. The human who directs them and validates their output takes both credit and responsibility.

### Qualifying Contributions

Authors should have made substantial contributions in at least one of the following areas (whether through direct implementation or skilled use of AI tools):

1. **Research Direction & Design**
   - Identifying key questions and conceptual approach
   - Developing theoretical frameworks or methodologies
   - Designing experiments, observations, or analysis strategies
   - Critical interpretation and synthesis of results

2. **Technical Solutions**
   - Creating essential analysis code or algorithms (directly or through AI)
   - Designing and validating data processing pipelines
   - Building research infrastructure or AI workflows that enable the work
   - Ensuring correctness and reproducibility of technical components

3. **Data & Domain Expertise**
   - Securing access to or generating essential datasets
   - Providing critical expertise on data interpretation, systematics, or instrumental effects
   - Performing and validating key analyses (directly or through AI)
   - Statistical design and uncertainty quantification

4. **Communication & Synthesis**
   - Crafting the manuscript narrative (directly or through AI)
   - Creating figures, visualizations, or presentations that convey key insights
   - Synthesizing literature to position the work
   - Ensuring clarity and correctness of the final output

### Authorship Expectations

For postdoc/student-led independent projects, the lead researcher should typically be first and corresponding author. The PI is not automatically included - authorship depends on actual intellectual contribution, not hierarchy or resource provision.

Author order generally follows: first author (lead researcher), middle authors (by contribution), last author (senior advisor when contributing).

### Non-Qualifying Contributions

The following, while valued, warrant acknowledgment rather than authorship:
- General encouragement or attendance at talks
- Routine administrative support
- Providing already-existing code/data without adaptation
- Minor proofreading or formatting
- General supervision without specific project input

## Student Supervision & Training

For early student work and projects discussed in weekly supervision meetings, the supervisor's advisory role typically merits authorship. These meetings are essential two-way interactions for learning the craft of research and represent substantial intellectual contribution.

This applies across all supervision relationships - PI/postdocs supervising PhD & Masters students, or PhD students supervising Masters students.

Masters projects naturally form research papers, while PhD theses work well as collections of papers with introduction and conclusion chapters added. The Cambridge system makes it difficult for the same chapter to appear in multiple theses. Therefore, student projects should be majority-owned by a single student to avoid conflicts when incorporating collaborative work into their thesis.

## Group Identity

While authorship is merit-based, **all papers produced by group members maintain the Handley Lab identity**. Papers by group members acknowledge the research environment and infrastructure of the Handley Lab, and are listed on our [papers page](/papers/).

## Open Science & Reproducibility

Open science is fundamental to our research philosophy. All papers include:
- **Preprints**: posted to arXiv or an equivalent repository
- **Code Repository**: all analysis code in version control (git), publicly accessible
- **Zenodo Archive**: a frozen archive containing everything needed to reproduce all figures and numbers in the paper (scripts, data/chains, environment specification, README)
- **Direct Reproducibility**: anyone (most importantly future you) should be able to regenerate all plots and quoted numbers from the archive
